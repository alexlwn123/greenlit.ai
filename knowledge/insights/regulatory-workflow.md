# Regulatory workflow

## Current synthesis

The emerging workflow is:

1. Define the product, process, intended use, and regulatory argument.
2. Assemble process descriptions, prior art, safety evidence, and internal artifacts.
3. Identify missing evidence and commission required studies.
4. Lock enough of the intended commercial process to produce representative batches.
5. Produce and analyze representative manufacturing batches.
6. Prepare a preliminary dossier and resolve internal inconsistencies.
7. Obtain final expert and legal review.
8. Submit and manage regulator questions or amendments.

## Contradictory or limiting evidence

- Ryan remembered Perfect Day's company/counsel workflow as relatively smooth and
  turnkey, so broad claims of coordination dysfunction are not yet supported.
- Perfect Day intentionally pursued a familiar regulatory argument; more novel or
  unsuccessful cases may have materially different workflows.

## Product implications

- Model technical dependencies and readiness, not merely document status.
- Maintain traceability from every substantive claim to supporting evidence.
- Preserve process versions so teams can see what changed and what evidence may
  need to be revisited.
- Present drafting as downstream of structured evidence and readiness.
- Keep final expert judgment and legal submission explicitly human-controlled.
