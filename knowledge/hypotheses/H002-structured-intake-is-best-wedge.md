# Structured intake is Greenlit's best initial wedge

## Claim

Companies will initially trust Greenlit to organize submission materials and
coordinate readiness before they trust it to generate substantive drafts or make
regulatory judgments.

## Evidence for

- Ryan said gathering sources, maintaining a workspace, and taking a first pass
  at a draft aligned with the problem.
- Intake and coordination create a lower-risk adoption path than replacing counsel.
- Structured evidence is a prerequisite for reliable drafting and gap analysis.

## Evidence against or unresolved

- Ryan did not report substantial coordination pain in Perfect Day's process.
- Existing document and project-management tools may be considered sufficient.
- No interviewee has yet demonstrated willingness to pay specifically for intake.

## Falsification criteria

- Active teams consistently report that evidence collection and readiness are
  already well managed.
- Drafting or gap analysis produces substantially greater urgency and willingness to pay.

## Next tests

- Ask teams to demonstrate their current workspace rather than only describe it.
- Identify the last missing, stale, or inconsistent artifact they discovered.
- Test a narrow readiness workspace with one active team.
